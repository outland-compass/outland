# RAFTER SPATIAL AND TECHNICAL BRIEF V1

> **Alignment note — 2026-09-07:** Updated to incorporate owner-approved Floor Plan V2 decisions. Where older V1 wording conflicted, the 2026-09-07 decisions supersede it.

## 1. Purpose
Authoritative spatial and technical brief for future plans, naval/build discussions and physical mock-ups. It intentionally avoids unverified engineering numbers.

## 2. Physical concept
RAFTER is a compact catamaran-based OUTLAND World designed around a transformable guest environment, direct contact with the river, equipment for self-directed activity, and an overnight configuration for up to eight single sleeping positions.

### Dimensions
- Compact ~8 m class concept: `ASSUMPTION`.
- Earlier larger concepts are `SUPERSEDED` as fixed specifications.
- Exact LOA, beam, freeboard, displacement, payload, structural system and draft: `TO VERIFY`.

## 3. Orientation vocabulary
All drawings must use:
- **BOW / PRAMAC** — forward.
- **STERN / KRMA** — aft.
- **PORT / LEVA STRANA** — vessel left when facing bow.
- **STARBOARD / DESNA STRANA** — vessel right when facing bow.

## 4. Mandatory spatial logic
From bow to stern, the plan must preserve these functional ideas even if exact dimensions change:
1. Forward guest / river-facing zone.
2. Transformable Inner Room / main protected guest volume.
3. Continuous central longitudinal corridor from Front Deck through River Room, Inner Room and Service Core to Dirty Deck/stern.
4. Compact service core with central corridor.
5. Minimal dirty/service working zone toward stern.
6. Two external stern boat-rack stacks, port and starboard.

The precise order/interface of helm, galley, WC/shower and service elements must be finalized by the physical design team, but circulation constraints below are mandatory.

## 5. Circulation — non-negotiable rules
- Passage through the Inner Room is intentional via the continuous central longitudinal corridor; guests must not need to cross sleeping surfaces, and the corridor must remain unobstructed in day, dining, night, service and emergency modes.
- The shortened service section must retain a **central corridor**.
- Staff/service movement should be kept away from sleeping positions as far as the compact footprint allows.
- Essential access and emergency egress must work when furniture is in night configuration.
- Passport, Mystery or powered locks must never be the sole means of safe exit.
- Dirty/wet operations must not require routing wet gear through sleeping surfaces.

## 5A. Main entrance and River/Inner boundary
### Main entrance — DECIDED
- The main physical entrance into the enclosed RAFTER volume is **Front Deck → automatic main entrance → River Room**.
- The automatic main entrance is controlled by Passport/NFC through the local entrance controller during normal guest access.
- Exact door type, actuator, lock, weather sealing, power architecture and emergency-release hardware are `TO VERIFY`.
- A mechanical/manual fail-safe or emergency-egress path independent of Passport/NFC, network/cloud and normal powered automation is mandatory.

### River Room ↔ Inner Room — DECIDED
- River Room and Inner Room are separated by a **lightweight sliding partition**.
- This partition is for spatial separation, privacy and day/night transformation; it is not the main security entrance and is not a heavy structural/security door.
- Its opening must preserve the continuous central corridor.
- Exact material, track, acoustic/privacy performance and construction are `TO VERIFY`.

### River Room dining — DECIDED
- River Room supports dining for 8.
- Dining tables and all 8 chairs are folding/removable so the room can return to open lounge/work/view/experience mode.
- Future drawings must not imply permanent dining furniture.

## 6. Inner Room
### Day mode — DECIDED
- Visually and physically opens strongly toward the river.
- Maximizes view, daylight and connection to surroundings.
- Supports lounge/social configuration using OUTLAND BLOCKS.

### Night / adverse-weather mode — DECIDED
Must provide:
- privacy;
- light isolation / blackout capability;
- thermal isolation appropriate for the intended season;
- rain and wind protection;
- insect protection where needed;
- ventilation without unacceptable condensation.

Exact system — rigid removable panels, roll systems, layered curtains/screens or hybrid: `TO VERIFY` by mock-up. The outcome is fixed; the construction method is not.

## 7. Forward guest zone
The forward zone is guest-first: river view, sitting, social use, arrival/orientation and day living. It should not become a permanent equipment store or service yard. Exact openness/roofing/closures: `TO VERIFY` in the final plan.

## 8. Helm / control position
A functional helm/driver position is required if the vessel is self-propelled. Exact position and visibility requirements: `TO VERIFY`. It must not obstruct the continuous central corridor or night-mode emergency circulation.

## 9. Service core
- Short/compact service core: `DECIDED`.
- Central corridor: `DECIDED`.
- Galley/kitchen, WC/shower, technical storage and operator functions must be packaged around that logic.
- Exact appliance list, tank interfaces, ventilation and dimensions: `TO VERIFY`.

## 10. Dirty Deck / stern service zone
The Dirty Deck is a minimal work/wet/service area, not permanent crew accommodation. It may support skipper/host tasks when present. It must enable handling of wet gear, basic service work and access to stern equipment without taking over the guest experience.

## 11. Boat rack specification — drawing-critical
There are exactly **two symmetric external stern racks**:
- one at the outer port side of the stern;
- one at the outer starboard side of the stern;
- each rack runs parallel to the catamaran's longitudinal line;
- each rack is a **vertical stack**.

Each rack contains, from top to bottom:
1. SUP board;
2. kayak;
3. tandem canoe.

### Top-view rule
A plan view must show the footprint/read of **one vertical stack on each side**. It must **not** draw SUP, kayak and canoe as three separate vessels lying next to each other on deck.

## 12. Activity equipment system
Core activity intent includes direct use of the river through SUP, kayak and tandem canoe equipment. Quantities implied by two identical racks are a design intent; final registered load and operational availability are `TO VERIFY`.

Required support systems:
- properly sized PFD/life jackets: `TO VERIFY` quantity/spec;
- paddles and leashes where appropriate;
- dry storage for guest valuables;
- wet gear drying zone;
- first-aid and rescue equipment;
- weather/water-level restrictions and operator guidance.

## 13. OUTLAND BLOCKS interface
### DECIDED
- 8 single modules.
- Day use: lounge/seating/daybed arrangements.
- Night use: 8 single sleeping positions.
- Backrest solution uses a removable external **L element behind the back**.
- Base and backrest contain sewn pockets/sleeves for the L element.
- No hidden docking hardware.
- No drilling through foam.
- Internal/removable covers plus **8 additional waterproof covers** for wet-use/day-lounger mode.

### TO VERIFY by 1:1 prototype
- module dimensions;
- foam type/density;
- comfort for overnight use;
- L-element section/material and load capacity;
- pocket reinforcement;
- fabric breathability, washability and drying;
- waterproof cover condensation/comfort;
- stacking/storage footprint;
- conversion time for one operator;
- final supplier quote.
