# RAFTER COMMERCIAL MODEL V1

> **Alignment note — 2026-09-07:** Updated to incorporate owner-approved Floor Plan V2 decisions. Where older V1 wording conflicted, the 2026-09-07 decisions supersede it.

## 1. Commercial thesis
RAFTER V1 is a low-CAPEX, high-distinctiveness OUTLAND pilot. It should validate whether a compact, transformable river World can create stronger demand and pricing than a generic floating rental while remaining operationally simple enough for largely uncrewed stays.

## 2. CAPEX discipline — DECIDED
- Target RAFTER V1 CAPEX: **€15,000**.
- Working envelope: **€15,000–€20,000**.
- Above €20,000: mandatory explicit re-underwriting/value engineering.
- **€25,000 is a stop/go ceiling, not an approved budget.**

Legacy €60–70k RAFTER/RIVERKEEPER V1 assumptions are `SUPERSEDED` for this pilot.

## 3. What CAPEX must ultimately include
Exact amounts remain `TO VERIFY` until itemized quotes exist:
- hull/catamaran/platform structure;
- superstructure/enclosure;
- interior/service core;
- WC/shower/water/waste systems;
- propulsion/navigation equipment;
- electrical/energy/HVAC;
- boat racks;
- SUP/kayak/canoe equipment;
- safety equipment;
- OUTLAND BLOCKS and 8 waterproof covers;
- Passport/SENSE prototype hardware, including local control hardware for the automatic main entrance;
- Cartographer cabinet/Mystery physical artifacts;
- berth/startup fees;
- contingency.

No modeled line item is a confirmed quotation unless attached evidence says so.

## 4. Base operating model
Preferred base case is **uncrewed/self-service** commercial stay, which avoids treating full-time host/skipper wages as unavoidable per-stay OPEX. This base case remains conditional on legal/safety verification.

Hosted experience and skipper-driven movement are optional/conditional costs.

## 5. Product and pricing
Primary sale unit should be a **RAFTER experience / mini-expedition**, potentially including overnight stay, rather than a commodity bed-night.

Exact target price: `ASSUMPTION / TO VERIFY` through competitor research and pilot demand testing.

Potential revenue components:
- core stay/experience;
- hosted food/cook package;
- skipper-led movement/river excursion where lawful;
- special event/private booking;
- optional guided activity layer.

V1 should not depend on many add-ons to make the core economics work.

## 6. Seasonality
Danube weather, temperature, daylight, water level and river conditions imply material seasonality. Exact sellable season and winter viability are `TO VERIFY`.

## 7. Scenario model
Because final pricing, berth cost, legal crew requirement and build quotes are still unverified, V1 scenarios are decision models rather than forecasts.

### Conservative
- CAPEX near upper working envelope.
- Shorter season.
- Low utilization.
- Mostly core stays, limited add-ons.
- Higher maintenance/turnover cost.
- Outcome expectation: validate product without assuming attractive ROI.

### Base
- CAPEX near target-to-mid working envelope.
- Uncrewed stay legally viable.
- Moderate seasonal utilization.
- Core experience commands a premium over generic raft accommodation.
- Outcome expectation: operational break-even and evidence for optimization/second World decisions.

### Optimistic
- CAPEX stays near target.
- Strong demand and repeat/referral effect.
- High but operationally sustainable seasonal utilization.
- Hosted/add-on revenue selectively improves margin.
- Outcome expectation: fast payback and strong evidence that OUTLAND experience layer creates economic value.

## 8. Required model inputs before numerical ROI is called reliable
- itemized build quote;
- berth cost and contract;
- insurance cost;
- utilities/waste cost;
- cleaning/turnover labor;
- local response/security cost;
- booking/channel commissions;
- maintenance reserve;
- legal crew/host cost if required;
- realistic average selling price;
- realistic occupied stays/expeditions by month;
- taxes/fees.

Until these are verified, any exact ROI or break-even date is an `ASSUMPTION`, not a project fact.

## 9. Break-even framework
Operational break-even occurs when contribution from stays/experiences covers recurring berth, utilities, cleaning, channel fees, maintenance reserve, local support, insurance and required staffing.

Investment payback additionally recovers total CAPEX. The project should calculate both separately once verified inputs exist.

## 10. Financial stop/go rules
- If validated minimum build scope cannot be delivered within €20k, re-underwrite before committing.
- If the required legal operating model forces permanent crew and materially destroys the base economics, re-underwrite the commercial proposition.
- If lawful supervised berth cannot be secured on acceptable terms, do not compensate by accepting an informal/high-risk berth.
- If safety/waste/registration requirements add unavoidable cost beyond the €25k stop/go ceiling, stop or redesign rather than silently expanding budget.

## 11. Commercial risks
1. Legal uncrewed model not permitted.
2. Build quote exceeds working envelope.
3. Berth cost/security weakens economics.
4. Season shorter than assumed.
5. Product priced like generic accommodation instead of an experience.
6. Turnover/maintenance burden higher than modeled.
7. Water/activity cancellations reduce utilization.
8. Third-party booking channels commoditize the product.

## 12. What V1 is trying to prove
Not maximum revenue. The first goal is evidence that:
- guests want the RAFTER experience;
- space transforms well for 8-design-capacity;
- uncrewed operation can be safe and manageable;
- Passport/Mystery add value without adding fragility;
- the product can stay inside disciplined CAPEX;
- real economics justify continued investment.
